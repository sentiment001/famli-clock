Maryland paid family leave (FAMLI): the employer guide

Maryland paid family leave is the state's FAMLI program. Employers pay in on wages paid from 1 January 2027, at 0.9 percent split between employer and employee. Employees can claim paid leave from January 2028.

This page is about Maryland FAMLI, administered by the Maryland Department of Labor, FAMLI Division. Colorado runs a separate paid leave program with the same name and different rules; nothing here applies to it.

The state publishes the rate. It does not publish your notice date or your first payment. Five questions get you both, plus a printable report.

One program, three names

The Maryland Department of Labor's name for it is Family and Medical Leave Insurance, FAMLI for short. Its own FAQ says people also call it paid family and medical leave, or just paid leave. FAMLI FAQ, question 1 You will also see PFML, short for paid family and medical leave. It is one program under one law: Title 8.3 of the Labor and Employment Article, with the details in COMAR 09.42. COMAR 09.42

It is not the paid sick days employers already give under the Healthy Working Families Act. The Department puts the difference simply: sick days are for an everyday cold, and FAMLI is for a serious illness. FAMLI FAQ, question 15 The two laws count employees differently too, which we cover in sick and safe leave vs FAMLI.

What it costs

The 2027 rate is 0.9 percent of wages, split 0.45 percent employer and 0.45 percent employee. FAMLI FAQ, question 21 Employers may take the employee half out of pay. They don't have to, and an employer can choose to pay it for everyone. Each November from 2027, the Department announces the next year's rate, and the law caps it at 1.2 percent. LE 8.3-601

In dollars: a 40-person employer with a $3.2 million Maryland payroll owes $28,800.00 for 2027. If it withholds the full employee half, $14,400.00 of that comes out of pay. The contribution rate page has the full working.

Small employers are not exempt

An employer with fewer than 15 employees pays no employer share. It still remits the 0.45 percent employee half, which it can take from pay or cover itself. FAMLI FAQ, question 21 The 15 counts everyone under the same federal EIN, in Maryland and in every other state. question 26 Exactly 15 is not small.

A 12-person employer on a $780,000 payroll remits $3,510.00 for 2027, and none of it is employer money. In 2027 the Department decides size quarter by quarter, so an employer that hires past 14 moves to the full rate from that quarter. And yes, a family that employs a nanny is an employer for FAMLI. question 55 More on the small employer rules.

Which wages count

Gross wages, using the same definition Maryland uses for unemployment insurance. Not net pay. FAMLI FAQ, questions 31 and 37 Wages count up to the Social Security wage base, per employee, per calendar year. That base is the 2027 Social Security taxable maximum of $192,300, which SSA published on 14 October 2026. The Department applies that cap separately to each job an employee holds. question 39 Details on the 2027 wage base page.

Only work done in Maryland counts. Someone who lives in Pennsylvania and works in Baltimore is covered. Someone who lives in Maryland and works full time in Virginia is not. question 53

What to do before January

  1. Register with the FAMLI Division. Every employer with at least one Maryland employee has to, whatever its size. MD Labor, registration Here is what you need to register.
  2. Decide about a private plan. The Declaration of Intent closes 15 November 2026. Once that date passes, the next step is a private plan application, due 1 October 2027. A private plan won't lower your 2027 cost, because the same contributions go into escrow instead. More on the Declaration of Intent page.
  3. Decide whether to withhold. A skipped deduction can't come out of a later paycheck. The one exception is an employee who earned too little in a pay period to cover it, a tipped worker for example; then you get up to 6 pay periods. FAMLI FAQ, question 40
  4. Send the written notice at least one pay period before deductions start. COMAR 09.42.02.05D So the date is different for every payroll calendar. The calculator works out yours, and the notice page explains why.
  5. Get ready to report. Each quarter you file gross wages and hours for every Maryland employee, on the FAMLI website rather than BEACON. The first report and payment are due 30 April 2027. questions 33 and 35

What employees get, from January 2028

Up to 12 weeks of paid leave in a benefit year. An employee who has their own serious health condition and also welcomes a child in the same year can get up to 24 weeks. FAMLI FAQ, question 96 Pay is up to 90 percent of wages, with a ceiling of $1,000 a week. questions 92 and 93

To qualify, an employee needs 680 hours of work in Maryland over the four calendar quarters reported before the claim or the leave, whichever comes first. Hours from more than one employer count. question 64 Leave can be used for a new child (birth, adoption or foster care), the employee's own serious health condition, a family member's serious health condition, or a family member's military deployment. question 69 The employer holds the job and keeps health benefits going during the leave. questions 105 and 106

Looking for paid maternity or parental leave in Maryland? FAMLI covers bonding with a new child, including adoption and foster care, from January 2028. Parents can take it any time in the first year after the birth or placement, and both parents can be on leave at the same time. A baby born in late 2027 still counts, as long as the leave is taken after benefits start and within that first year. FAMLI FAQ, questions 71 and 72 Until then, paid time off for a new child comes from your employer's own policy, or from earned sick and safe leave, which Maryland lets you use for maternity or paternity leave. Questions

Does Maryland have paid family leave?

Yes. Maryland's Family and Medical Leave Insurance program, FAMLI, is paid family and medical leave. Contributions start on wages paid from 1 January 2027 and benefits start in January 2028.

When does Maryland paid family leave start?

Contributions apply to wages paid from 1 January 2027, and the first quarterly payment is due 30 April 2027. Benefits begin in January 2028.

Is Maryland paid family leave the same as FAMLI?

Yes. FAMLI is the official name of Maryland's paid family and medical leave program, run by the Maryland Department of Labor. Colorado has a separate program with the same acronym.

Do small businesses pay Maryland paid family leave?

Yes. With fewer than 15 employees, counting every state under one EIN, there is no employer share, but the business still remits the 0.45 percent employee share. It can take that from pay or cover it itself.

Is Maryland paid family leave deducted from paychecks?

It can be. Employers may withhold up to 0.45 percent of wages, but they don't have to. A skipped deduction can't be taken from a later paycheck, except when the employee earned too little that pay period to cover it.
